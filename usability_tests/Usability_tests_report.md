# Usability Testing — Consolidated Cross-Participant Report

**Scheduling System — Agenda Module | May 2026**

---

## 1. Overview

Four participants completed usability testing sessions under the think-aloud protocol during May 2026. Sessions ranged from 16 to 19 minutes. Five usability metrics (M-01 through M-05) were evaluated against pre-defined thresholds. No participant met more than two thresholds, and three metrics failed across all four participants. Elizabeth Delgado Cuevas was the strongest overall performer, meeting M-02 and M-04. Karla Medina and Selene Romero showed the weakest performance, each meeting zero thresholds.

## 10. Key Findings

1. **No participant met M-01, M-03, or M-05.** These three metrics — avoidable errors, clean completion rate, and autonomous recovery — represent the most critical and systemic usability failures.

2. **Elizabeth Delgado is the sole participant to meet any threshold for confidence (M-04).** Her prior experience with similar systems appears to be the differentiating factor; the interface does not self-evidently build confidence for unfamiliar users.

3. **UC-AG-03 (cancellation) is the only consistently smooth flow.** All participants who performed it completed it independently, suggesting its visual design is a viable reference pattern for other flows.

4. **Filter controls are the most widely problematic feature** (3 of 4 participants affected), yet the current design provides no onboarding, progressive disclosure, or visual affordance to guide discovery.

5. **The rescheduling flows (individual and bulk) are the highest source of frustration and task failure**, affecting 3 of 4 participants with symptoms ranging from full restarts to permanent confusion.

6. **Experience with similar systems is a stronger predictor of performance than general digital literacy.** This means the system's learnability depends heavily on transfer from other tools, rather than its own self-explanatory design — a significant risk for the target user population.
